Three-Centered Arch Porch

707 South 4th Street, Hamilton, MT · Curb Appeal / Porch Rebuild

← Back to Portfolio

This client has a sharp eye for detail and wanted something more distinctive than a standard porch rebuild. He came to us with the idea of a three-centered arch — a graceful, flattened arch style, sometimes called a basket-handle arch, that's been a signature of high-end Federal and Georgian doorways since the Renaissance. From there we drew the plans and worked out every detail of how to actually build it: the old porch was demoed, the arch truss and curved soffit were framed and built from scratch, and the whole thing was finished to a high level of detail the client specifically asked for.

It ended up being as much a curb-appeal upgrade as anything — the finished arch and soffit are the signature feature of the house, visible from the street. It's a good example of the kind of custom architectural detail we can draft, engineer, and build in-house, not just frame from a stock plan.

Fun fact: the three-centered arch

That graceful, flattened arch over the porch isn't a simple semicircle — it's a three-centered arch, sometimes called a “basket-handle arch” for its resemblance to the curve of a woven basket handle. Instead of one radius, it's built from three separate circular arcs (a wide, shallow one in the middle and two tighter ones at the springs) that blend together smoothly, with no visible kink where one curve hands off to the next. Architects have leaned on this trick since the Renaissance because it gets you the elegant, low-profile curve of an ellipse using nothing but a compass and straightedge. It became a signature of Federal and Georgian doorways and windows in early American architecture — part of why it still feels so at home on a historic Montana porch.
