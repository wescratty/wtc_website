Spacing Calculator

Board and baluster spacing, done the way each job actually gets laid out.

Spacing Calculator

There isn't one "board spacing" problem -- decking, balusters, rafter tails, and ceiling boards all get laid out differently, and the thing that matters (a code-max gap, an overhang you'll trim later, a rip you don't want too thin) changes with the job. Pick the one below that matches what you're doing. Not sure? Use "Compare all six" and fill in a couple to see how they differ.

Decking — Even Reveal

Use this when: both edges of the deck field are free (a picture-frame border, fascia board, or open edge will cover whatever the end gap comes out to). This solves for one consistent gap that repeats between every board AND at both ends -- the most common decking layout.

Actual width, not nominal -- a 5/4x6 or 2x6 board is usually 5 1/2" actual.

The calculator looks for a board count whose resulting gap lands between your target and this upper limit -- decking gaps are commonly 3/16" to 1/4".

Decking — Overhang & Trim

Use this when: one edge is fixed (against the house/ledger) and the other is a free outer edge (rim joist, fascia) you'll snap a line across and trim flush after the boards are down. This does not solve for an exact-fit gap -- it tells you how many boards you need and how much will hang past the edge to be trimmed off.

A fixed gap this time, not a range -- this method doesn't adjust the gap to fit.

Decking — Minimum Starter Rip

Use this when: one edge butts a wall and the layout needs a ripped starter board against it. This finds a gap (within your range) that keeps that starter board at or above a minimum width, instead of leaving you with an unusably thin, weak sliver against the wall.

Common rule of thumb: don't rip a board down to less than about 2".

Stair & Deck Balusters — Code Max Gap

Use this when: you need to fit balusters (or slats) between two fixed posts/newels while keeping every gap at or under a code-maximum spacing. Maximizes baluster count for the tightest legal gap, and also shows a couple of slightly airier (but still legal) alternatives.

IRC guards are commonly a 4" sphere rule (max gap 4"); stair guards sometimes allow up to 4 3/8". Always confirm your local code.

Decorative Rafter Tails — On Layout

Use this when: adding decorative (non-structural) rafter tails to a fascia and you want them to land on real on-center stations -- like the actual rafter layout, or every other/third station of it -- so they read as structural framing instead of evenly-smeared decoration. This isn't a "fill the gap" problem: it lists the actual station marks, since a real on-center layout almost never divides evenly into the wall length.

Leave at 1 to match the real rafter layout exactly, or use 2/3/etc. for a sparser decorative pattern (e.g. every other rafter).

Tongue & Groove Ceiling Boards

Use this when: laying T&G board with no visible gap by design. This packs boards edge to edge and checks whether the final board against the wall comes out wide enough to be worth installing, both as a single rip at one wall and split evenly at both walls.

Face-to-face reveal width, not the nominal size -- a "1x6" T&G profile typically covers about 5 1/8" to 5 1/2" depending on the mill. Measure a sample board if you're not sure.
